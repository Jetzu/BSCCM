## 1. Group-level (slide/FOV) label offsets
- **MiDaS** (depth; datasets with incompatible scale and shift). https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01341: "We develop tools that enable mixing multiple datasets during training, even if their annotations are incompatible", "a robust training objective that is invariant to changes in depth range and scale". The robust version uses median as shift and mean absolute deviation as scale, and "trimmed MAE loss yields the lowest validation error over all datasets" (the margin is only shown in a figure). **Project:** the shift-only within-FOV pairwise loss is already flat (N061: .6742 vs control .6706). Labels are already per-slide z-scores, so slide-level affine invariance is close to a no-op.
- **Random effects.** ARMED, https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11783: "ARMED improves accuracy on data from clusters seen during training (up to 28% vs. conventional models)" "and generalization to unseen clusters (up to 9% vs. conventional models)." LMMNN (NeurIPS 2021) reports that treating categorical features as random effects gives "a significant improvement in prediction performance" [snippet]. **Project:** the per-FOV random intercept (`--fov-re`) scored .6740 (flat, K042) and is already inside the E8 tpfov members.
- **Test-time adaptation for regression.** SSA (ICLR 2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03263: "naive feature alignment employed in existing TTA methods for classification is ineffective or even worse for regression". It adapts "with unlabeled target data" (no numbers in the abstract). GOPSA (NeurIPS 2024), https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03878, learns "a domain-specific re-centering operator representing site-specific intercepts" [snippet]. Both are **transductive, so DECLINED**. AdaBN already hurt (-.005 to -.011, K023).
- **CHRep (2026, leave-one-slide-out).** https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21573 names "slide-level appearance shifts" as the problem. Its loss is MSE + λ·MAE + λ_PCC(1−PCC̄), with PCC̄ "the average gene-wise Pearson correlation computed across the mini-batch" and "The PCC-loss coefficient is set to 0.3". Removing its regression term costs only 0.3522 → 0.3482 (+.0040). Its kNN-gallery calibration step adds +.0092 to +.056. **Project:** the Pearson loss scored .6726 (flat) and kNN decoders were flat (N087).
- **Verdict:** every inductive tool the literature offers for group offsets (random intercepts, within-group or shift-invariant losses, Pearson loss) has already been tried here and is flat. The literature's answer to a shift in the test domain's labels is transductive test-time adaptation, which is declined.

## 2. Censored (clip-floor) targets
- Svensson et al. 2024/25, https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04313v1: "for censored labels we use the integral of the probability density function that is within the correct censored region". Left-censored labels use "the negative logarithm of the cumulative distribution function". The approach "enhanced Gaussian models in cases where >35% of the data contained censoring".
- sciPENN (Nat Mach Intell 2022) merges protein panels that only partly overlap "using a censored loss" [snippet].
- **Project:** N073 bundled the upper-bound loss with 20 epochs and scored .6658 (−.0048). CD19 gained +.03 and b10 lost from the longer schedule. The censored loss alone at 10 epochs was never run (parked). b01 trains on draw 0, where grp:CD16 is 30.7% censored, close to Svensson's 35% regime.

## 3. Correlation structure with partially observed outputs
- **totalVI**, https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.05.08.083337v2.full-text: "the naive calculation of correlations on expected denoised values generated false positive correlations". It uses "a novel sampling method that controls for nuisance variation while avoiding denoising-induced artifacts". Its priors are batch-specific: "By being batch specific, these priors ... account for differences". On a held-out panel, "the mean error of totalVI was better than the Seurat v3 error for approximately 68% of the 80 proteins".
- **Zhang, Atwal & Lim, Patterns 2021** (Europe PMC PMC7961184): "all the methods except NormUMI generated inflated gene-gene correlations". Median ρ was NBR .839, MAGIC .789, DCA .770 and SAVER .166, against NormUMI .023. Their fix adds uniform noise up to each gene's 1st percentile and recovered 99.0-, 50.9- and 31.6-fold PPI enrichment for the top 100 / 1,000 / 10,000 pairs (NBR).
- **Infinity Flow** (sparse exploratory markers imputed from a backbone), https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.06.17.152926v2.full: "any pattern captured by Infinity Flow has an underlying signature in the Backbone data". Background correction "removed spurious correlation patterns within non-expressing cells". Nonlinear models reached "a median AUC between 0.88 and 0.89" against "median AUC of 0.71" for linear. Here the image plays the backbone's role, so co-expression that the image does not carry cannot be decoded.
- **HistoPlexer** (Nat Mach Intell 2025; PMC12364712): "The MP setting consistently outperforms SP across all methods". For marker-pair Spearman co-localization (pairs with |SCC|>0.15), "The MP model achieves an MSE approximately an order of magnitude lower than that of the SP model". This is a GAN, i.e. generative.
- **2026 spatial-transcriptomics work:** CorrFlow, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22187, notes that existing methods "treat genes as independent prediction targets" and adds a gene-affinity graph. It is flow-based (declined family) and reports "best average PCC and HPCC" with no structure numbers.
- **Low-rank Gaussian copula output heads.** Salinas et al. 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03002: low-rank covariance plus a copula for non-Gaussian marginals, scaling to "thousands" of series [snippet]. A Gaussian negative log-likelihood (NLL) can be marginalised to whatever subset of outputs is observed. Pairs that are never measured together are identified only through the low-rank factor.
- **Project:** in the N096 quick run, a correlation-matching loss at λ=1 cut err_all from .391 to .362 (b10) and .340 to .286 (b01), but GOAL fell by .024 and .028. In b01 the error sits in single:X~autofl pairs (.421), not the pooled pairs (.123), and those label correlations flip sign between draws. That part is acquisition artefact and should not be matched.
- **Literature consensus:** do not bend the predicted means toward the label correlations. Get co-expression from the predictive distribution (means plus modelled residual) and evaluate it within-type with nuisance removed.

## Ideas
1. **Correlation from the predictive distribution, not the means.**
   - Mechanism: fit the residual covariance of E8 on validation FOVs from the source draw, within gated type, after centring each FOV. Use a low-rank factor fitted on co-measured pairs. Add sampled residuals to the E8 means, or noise-regularise as in Zhang 2021, then compute the correlation error. Report the error separately on FOV-centred labels.
   - Why it moves the bottleneck: totalVI and Zhang show that correlations of expected values are inflated by construction. This fixes the user's correlation error without touching the means, so GOAL is unchanged.
   - Expected gain: err_pooled should fall well (inflation component). err_single~autofl will not, because the draw-specific sign flip is out of reach inductively.
   - Falsifier: 0 GPU-h, CPU on the saved E8 predictions. Pass = err_all down ≥30% with GOAL identical.
   - Build: about 80 lines.
2. **Censored loss alone, at 10 epochs** (N073's parked arm), on all censored targets.
   - Mechanism: Tobit or one-sided upper-bound loss in place of MSE on cells at the clip floor.
   - Why it moves the bottleneck: MSE fits the draw-0 floor mass (grp:CD16 .307) as exact values, while the test draw has a different censoring mix (.082).
   - Expected gain: small, about +.002 to +.005 on b01 (CD19 +.03 ≈ +.003 on the mean). Unlikely to reach +.0054 alone; screen it as an ensemble member.
   - Falsifier: one r50 pair (b10 + b01, ≈46+32 min ≈1.3 GPU-h). Pass = b01 grp:CD16/CD19 up with b10 unchanged.
   - Build: about 0, the flag exists from N073.
3. **Group-specific residual variance (label-reliability weighting).**
   - Mechanism: this is the mixed-model extension that LMMNN/ARMED leave out (σ²_e per group, not only an intercept). Estimate each training slide's and replicate's label reliability per target from existing within-draw validation-FOV predictions. Weight the loss by 1/σ²_g, with shrinkage.
   - Why it moves the bottleneck: K043 shows replicate slides from the same draw differ .881 vs .578. Random intercepts (flat) only absorb offsets, not slides whose labels are noisy throughout.
   - Expected gain: uncertain, +0 to +.006 on b01.
   - Falsifier: step one costs 0 GPU-h. If the per-slide validation r varies by less than .1 within each target, drop the idea. Otherwise run one r50 pair, about 1.3 GPU-h.
   - Build: about 40 lines.
